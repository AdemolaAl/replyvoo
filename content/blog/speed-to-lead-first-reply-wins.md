Picture a customer scrolling at 10:47pm. She sees your ad for a wig, taps through to Telegram and types "How much?" She also messages two other sellers she saw earlier.

One replies in eight seconds with the price, a photo and a question about her preferred length. One replies at 7:30am. You reply at noon, after your morning errands.

Who do you think got the sale?

This is **speed to lead**: the idea that the time between a customer's first message and your first useful reply has a huge effect on whether they buy. In chat commerce, it might be the single most underrated lever you have.

## Why speed matters so much in chat

Intent is perishable. The moment someone messages you is the moment they care most. Every minute after, something else competes for their attention: another seller, a WhatsApp group, a video, dinner, sleep.

A few first principles:

- **Interest peaks at first contact.** They just saw your product. They want to know now.
- **Chat buyers often shop around.** Messaging three sellers costs nothing. The first good answer sets the anchor.
- **A fast reply signals reliability.** If you answer quickly before the sale, they assume you'll deliver quickly after it.
- **Momentum compounds.** A quick back-and-forth builds a rhythm. A six-hour gap breaks it, and you're starting over.

> The first reply isn't just an answer. It's a preview of what doing business with you feels like.

## Where businesses lose speed

Most sellers aren't slow because they don't care. They're slow because of structure:

- **Nights and weekends.** Ads keep running. You don't.
- **Busy hours.** A rush of messages after a post or Blast means some wait.
- **One person bottleneck.** The owner is the only one who knows the prices.
- **Repetitive questions.** You type the same answer forty times a day and fall behind.
- **Paid traffic spikes.** An ad starts performing and the inbox floods.

That last one hurts most. You're paying for every click. Leads that wait hours are ad spend going to waste.

## Fast is not enough. Fast and useful.

A fast "Hi, how can I help?" isn't speed to lead. It's a delay with extra steps. The customer already told you what they wanted.

A good first reply:

1. **Answers the question asked.** Price, availability, delivery.
2. **Adds one useful detail.** A photo, a size guide, a popular option.
3. **Asks one question that moves the sale forward.** "Which length do you prefer?" "Where should we deliver?"

For example, to "How much?" on a hypothetical wig store:

"Hi! This bone straight wig is ₦85,000 for 16 inches and ₦110,000 for 20 inches. Both come with a free cap. Which length are you thinking of?"

That reply answers, informs and moves forward, in one message.

## Measuring your own speed

Don't take anyone's word for it, including ours. Test it on your own business:

1. Look at last month's chats. Note how long first replies took, especially at night and on weekends.
2. Note how many of those leads bought.
3. Turn on a Closer for a few weeks.
4. Compare the same numbers.

Use your Command Center and the Leads view to track how many conversations move from New to Won. Ask **Juno**: "How many leads came in outside business hours this week, and how many reached Checkout?"
