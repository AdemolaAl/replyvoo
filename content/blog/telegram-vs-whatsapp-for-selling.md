"Should we sell on Telegram or WhatsApp?" We hear it every week. The honest answer is: it depends on your customers, your offer and how you plan to grow. Both are excellent sales channels. They're just good at different things.

This comparison is written by a company that is **live on Telegram today** and **building for WhatsApp**, so we have an interest in both. We'll stick to how each platform actually works for sellers.

## The short answer

- **Choose WhatsApp first** if your customers are local, already message businesses on WhatsApp, and you sell one-to-one: shops, salons, clinics, real estate, local services.
- **Choose Telegram first** if you build a community or audience around your offer, sell to many people at once, or run content-led funnels: courses, coaching, trading education, creators, crypto and digital products.
- **Use both** when you can. They complement each other, and one AI agent can serve both.

Now the details.

## 1. Reach: where your customers already are

**WhatsApp** is the default messaging app for billions of people and, in many countries, the way people talk to businesses. If you sell in Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, South Africa, India, Brazil or much of Europe and Latin America, there's a good chance your customers already have WhatsApp open all day.

**Telegram** has a large global user base too, and in some markets and niches it's where communities live: trading and investing, crypto, tech, education, gaming and many creator audiences.

What to do: ask your last 20 customers which app they'd rather use. That beats any global statistic.

## 2. One-to-one selling

Both apps handle private chats well. Customers message you, you answer, you send a link.

**WhatsApp** feels personal and familiar. Customers trust a business with a verified name and profile. For local, high-trust purchases, that familiarity converts.

**Telegram** makes one-to-one selling easy with **bots**. A customer taps a link, starts your bot and lands in a chat with your sales agent. With **Telegram Business** (it needs Telegram Premium), an AI can even answer from your personal account, for people who prefer to "talk to the owner".

## 3. Groups, channels and communities

This is Telegram's biggest advantage.

- **Telegram channels** can broadcast to very large audiences, with no practical subscriber limit.
- **Telegram groups** support very large communities, with admin tools, topics and bots that moderate and answer questions.
- People can join with one link without sharing their phone number with everyone.

**WhatsApp** has groups and Channels, but they're built for smaller, closer circles, and WhatsApp Channels are a one-way feed rather than a sales conversation.

If your model is "build an audience, then sell to it", Telegram is hard to beat.

## 4. Automation and AI

**Telegram** is open to automation by design. Anyone can create a bot with @BotFather in a minute and connect it to software. There's no approval process for a standard bot and no per-message fee from Telegram. Bots can't message people first, which keeps things spam-free: the customer always starts the conversation.

**WhatsApp** automation runs through the **WhatsApp Business Platform**, Meta's official API. It's robust, but it comes with requirements:

- A Meta Business portfolio, a registered number and an approved display name, with business verification recommended
- The **24-hour customer service window**: free-flowing replies for 24 hours after the customer's last message, then only approved **template messages**
- Template messages are **paid**, per message, at Meta's rates by category and country
- Meta's current rules allow bots that do a business's own sales and support, not general-purpose AI assistants

Neither is "better". Telegram is quicker to start and cheaper to run at volume. WhatsApp has more rules but meets many customers where they already are. We cover the WhatsApp side in depth in our [WhatsApp AI chatbot guide](/blog/whatsapp-ai-chatbot-for-business-guide).

## 5. Broadcasts and follow-ups

**Telegram:** a bot can send messages to everyone who started it, at no cost from Telegram. You still need to respect people: send things they asked for and include a way to stop. Channels are made for broadcasting.

**WhatsApp:** messages to people outside the 24-hour window must use approved templates, sent only to people who opted in, and each one costs money. Done well, that's a strength: customers trust WhatsApp because businesses can't spam them freely.

If your growth depends on frequent broadcasts (new drops, daily content, alerts), Telegram is more practical. If you send occasional, high-value updates (order shipped, appointment tomorrow), WhatsApp's model is fine.

## 6. Payments

Both work well with **payment links**: your checkout page, a card link, or a local provider. On either app, an AI agent can send the link at the right moment and confirm what happens next.

Telegram also has built-in payment features for bots, including Telegram Stars for digital goods. WhatsApp Pay and in-chat payments exist only in some countries. For most sellers, a payment link from your own provider is the simplest option on both apps.

## 7. Privacy and trust

**Telegram** lets customers interact through usernames, so they don't have to share their phone number with your whole group. Some audiences value that.

**WhatsApp** is tied to phone numbers, which feels more "real" to many buyers and makes a business feel accountable. End-to-end encryption is on by default for personal chats; business conversations through the API are handled under Meta's business terms.

## 8. Costs, side by side

Here's how running costs compare (platform fees only, not your software subscription):

- **Telegram:** no fee from Telegram to run a bot, reply or broadcast. Telegram Premium is needed only if you want AI replies on your personal account through Telegram Business.
- **WhatsApp:** replies inside the 24-hour window are not charged the same way as templates; template messages outside the window are charged per message by Meta, varying by category and country.

So at high volume, Telegram is usually cheaper to run. WhatsApp's extra cost buys you reach in markets where everyone is already on it.

## A quick decision guide

Answer these five questions:

1. **Where do your customers already message businesses?** Go there first.
2. **Do you sell one-to-one or one-to-many?** One-to-one leans WhatsApp; one-to-many leans Telegram.
3. **Do you need a community?** Telegram.
4. **How often do you broadcast?** Often leans Telegram; occasionally works on either.
5. **How fast do you need to launch?** Telegram bots can be live today; WhatsApp needs Meta setup first.

## Why not both?

Many businesses run both: WhatsApp for local buyers and customer service, Telegram for the community, launches and content. The hard part isn't choosing; it's answering both inboxes fast enough.

That's where an AI agent helps. Whichever app the message comes from, the customer gets the same fast, accurate answer, the same qualifying questions and the same payment link, and your team sees every conversation in one inbox.
