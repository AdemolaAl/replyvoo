"Should I hire another person to answer chats, or use AI?" It's one of the most common questions business owners ask once their inbox gets busy. The honest answer: it depends on your numbers. So let's work out your numbers.

This guide gives you a simple way to calculate what manual chat support really costs you, compares it fairly with AI Closers, and shows the hybrid model most growing businesses end up with.

## The visible cost of manual support

Start with what you already know. For each person who answers chats, add up:

- Monthly salary
- Data and airtime allowance
- Phone or laptop
- Transport or remote work stipend
- Any commission or bonus
- Your time spent training and supervising them

That's the visible cost. Most owners stop here. Don't.

## The hidden costs

Manual support has costs that never show up on a payslip:

**Hours of coverage.** One person works maybe 8 to 10 hours a day, 5 or 6 days a week. Customers message 24/7. Covering nights and weekends means more people, shifts, or simply losing those leads.

**Slow replies at peak times.** When a post goes viral or an ad takes off, one person can only type so fast. The queue grows and leads cool. We cover why that's expensive in [Speed to lead: why the first reply wins](/blog/speed-to-lead-first-reply-wins).

**Inconsistent answers.** Different staff quote different delivery times. Someone forgets the current promo. Customers notice.

**Turnover.** When a trained agent leaves, their knowledge leaves too. You recruit and train again.

**Your own time.** If you're the owner and also the backup for every chat, your hours have a cost: the strategy, sourcing and marketing work you're not doing.

**Missed sales.** The hardest cost to see, and often the biggest. Leads that waited too long and bought elsewhere.

> The most expensive message is the one nobody answered.

## A simple formula

Here's a way to estimate your cost per conversation with manual support:

```
Monthly cost of chat staff (all-in)
÷ Conversations they handle per month
= Cost per conversation
```

Imagine a hypothetical online electronics shop. Fill it in with your own numbers:

- All-in monthly cost of chat staff: **your number**
- Conversations handled per month: **your number** (count unique customers chatting per day, times working days)

Then add a rough estimate of **missed conversations**: messages that arrived when no one was available, or waited hours. Even if you can't count them precisely, look at messages that came in after hours last month.

## What AI does better

- **Speed.** Replies in seconds, any time.
- **Coverage.** Nights, weekends and holidays included.
- **Volume.** A rush of 300 messages doesn't create a queue.
- **Consistency.** Same accurate answer every time, from the same knowledge base.
- **No retraining.** Update the knowledge base once and every Closer knows.

## What humans do better

- **Judgement in unusual situations.** Disputes, exceptions, custom deals.
- **Empathy in hard moments.** An upset customer often wants a person.
- **Relationships with big clients.** VIPs want to know you.
- **Negotiation.** Bulk orders and partnerships.
- **Improving the system.** Spotting patterns and fixing the knowledge base.

The point isn't AI *or* humans. It's putting each where they're strongest.

## The hybrid model

Most businesses that get this right land somewhere like this:

1. **Closers handle the front line.** Every first reply, every routine question, every after-hours lead. Ace for sales, Mira for support.
2. **Smart handoff routes the hard chats.** Angry customers, refunds, big orders go to a human in **Pulse Inbox**.
3. **Humans focus on Hot leads.** Use **HeatScore** to find people at Checkout and close them personally.
4. **One person owns quality.** They read the **Night Shift Report**, review chats and update knowledge weekly.

In this model, your existing staff stop being typists and become closers and quality managers. Often that means you don't need to hire for the next stage of growth, or the person you hire does higher-value work.

## Running the numbers on your business

Use this checklist:

1. Count conversations per month (including after hours).
2. Calculate your all-in manual cost per conversation.
3. Estimate how many conversations went unanswered or answered late.
4. Pick the Replyvoo plan that fits your volume.
5. Work out the AI cost per conversation.
6. Decide which chats still need a human, and how many hours that takes.
7. Compare total monthly cost and coverage, side by side.

Then test it. Start on the free Spark plan, or Starter for more volume, and run it alongside your current team for a few weeks. Compare real results, not estimates.

## Questions to ask yourself

- How many hours a day is someone available to reply?
- What happens to messages that arrive at 2am?
- How long does it take to train a new chat agent?
- How often do customers get different answers from different staff?
- What would your best person do if they didn't have to answer "how much?" fifty times a day?

## A note on quality

Cheaper isn't better if quality drops. Watch your Closers closely in the first weeks. Read chats in Pulse Inbox, ask **Juno** where customers got stuck, and keep refining. A well-trained Closer with human backup should leave customers happier than a slow inbox, not just cheaper to run.
